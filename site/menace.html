Teach a pile of matchboxes to play

MENACE keeps one matchbox per position and one colour of bead per move. To move, it draws a bead at random, so moves with more beads are likelier. After each game every move it made gains 3 beads for a win and 1 for a draw, and loses 1 for a loss. Boards that are rotations or reflections of each other share a box. Things to try

Who you learn from matters

Train only against Random, then switch the opponent to Perfect play and keep going: the losses come back. The benchmark found that MENACE trained on random play never stops losing to a perfect player (57% non-loss after 5,000 games). Trained on a mix, it reaches 100% within about 2,000 games.

Why it can't scale

Switch to (4,4,4). The box count keeps climbing and the curve barely moves. On (6,6,4) the tables in the benchmark had seen only 28 to 50% of the positions they met after 3,000 games, and they lost to the rule-based player every time.

Be the trainer

Use Play against it after a few hundred games. MENACE keeps learning from your games, just as Michie's did. If you beat it the same way twice, the second time it has fewer beads for the moves that lost.
