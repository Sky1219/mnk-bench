What to expect

Results

  • Tic-tac-toe is a draw with good play, so nearly every pairing of competent agents draws, and standings bunch up around a score of 0.5. Random is the exception.
  • Bigger boards spread the agents out. In our runs on (6,6,4), alpha-beta depth 4 beat MCTS (1,000 playouts) every game, and MENACE lost to rule-based every game: it has met almost none of the positions.
  • Moving first matters on gomoku and Connect Four. In our gomoku runs, alpha-beta depth 4 against depth 2 was won by whoever moved first. Keep alternate who moves first on so each player gets both sides.
  • Small samples are noisy. With 10 games a score can be off by ±0.3; use 50 or more games before trusting a difference.

Speed

  • Each game runs on one core. Games in parallel sets how many run at once. More than your core count doesn't help, and the default leaves one core free for the page.
  • Tic-tac-toe games take milliseconds. Alpha-beta games on gomoku take about 0.1–0.3 s each, and Connect Four is faster.
  • The 5 s thinking MCTS setting and minimax with a 15 s budget can make one game take minutes; on big boards minimax refuses and plays alpha-beta.
  • MENACE trains once per board, side and training length in each worker, so the first game in each worker starts more slowly. Training time is not counted in its time per move.
  • Every matchup gets its own random seed, so adding the same matchup again plays different games. Results stay in this tab until you clear them or download the CSV.
